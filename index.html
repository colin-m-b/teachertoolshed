Teacher Toolshed

Six small tools I built for my own middle and high school classes.

No. 01
Pure Writer

Writing with no spellcheck, grammar fixes, or pasting from outside. In-class writing, warts and all, so you can see what needs to improve.

Exports MLA PDF or Word Open →
No. 02
Presentation Tracker

Make notes, time the talking, and score against your rubric while they present, with per-student overrides so group grades stay honest.

Paste in your rubric Open →
No. 03
Stack Splitter

QR coversheets on handwritten work, one pass through the copier, and then split the pile by student. Digitize and itemize paperwork faster.

One named PDF per student Open →
No. 04
Talk Tracker

Tap a name during a Socratic seminar or a debate and track speaking contributions. Leave with a record and notes as evidence, instead of a vague sense of who carried it.

No setup — load a roster Open →
No. 05
Seating Chart Maker

Put students where you want them in your room. Randomize, swap names, and set up islands.

Prints to PDF Open →
No. 06
Hexagonal Activity Creator

Students connect concepts, terms, characters, etc., their own way

A note from me

I teach middle and high school English. I couldn't find the tools that I wanted, so I made them myself. I build them for my own classes first — which is why they're small, fast, and simple.

Nothing leaves your browser, and there's no account to make. The data and student info stays with you. Start by building your class lists — every tool reuses them.
